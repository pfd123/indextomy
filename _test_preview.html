When anxiety is not really about anything

Anxiety is rarely just a thinking problem. It is a nervous system doing its best to protect you, long after the original danger has passed. That is why it can feel so unreasonable: nothing obvious is wrong, yet your body is braced for something. Logic alone rarely reaches it, which is why reassurance and thinking your way out tend to wear off by evening.

Working somatically means we pay attention to that bracing directly. Rather than only asking why you feel anxious, we notice how it shows up: the breath, the posture, the flutter under the ribs. The aim is not to fight the anxiety but to help your system learn, slowly and safely, that the present moment is tolerable.

How Somatic Experiencing approaches anxiety

Somatic Experiencing was developed by Dr Peter Levine. It works with the body's natural capacity to regulate itself, gently guiding the nervous system out of permanent alert and back into ease. We work in small, tolerable steps, never pushing you into overwhelm, and everything moves at the speed your system sets.

There is no force and no drama in this. People often describe the relief as a quiet discovery: the shoulders dropping, the breath deepening on its own. That is the system completing what it got stuck on. I have worked this way for eight years, and I bring a person-centred foundation to it, so the relationship between us is part of the work too.

People often arrive expecting to talk about the anxiety itself. Usually we end up talking about the tight shoulders that came with it instead, and that tends to be where things start to shift.

Questions about anxiety and somatic work

Is somatic therapy effective for anxiety?

Many people find it reaches anxiety that talking alone has not shifted, because it works directly with the nervous system rather than only with thoughts. It is steady, paced work rather than a quick fix, and the free intro call is a good place to ask how it might fit you.

Can somatic therapy work online?

Yes. Sessions are held online across the UK and Ireland, and the body-focused work translates well to video. You control the pace throughout, and there is never any pressure to go further than feels steady.

What if talking about anxiety makes it worse?

That worry makes complete sense, and it is exactly what this way of working is designed for. We stay within your window of tolerance and work at the speed your system sets. Nothing is forced, and you can stop at any point.
