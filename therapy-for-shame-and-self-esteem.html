Understanding shame and the inner critic

Where guilt says you made a mistake, shame makes the painful claim that you are the mistake. It is an intensely physical sensation, often experienced as a heavy slump in the posture, a turning inward, or a burning sensation of wanting to disappear. High achievements or external success rarely quieten this internal sense of deficiency.

The inner critic often arises as a defensive shield, attempting to criticize you internally before anyone else has the chance to reject you. While its origin was self-protection, living with an relentless internal referee damages self-esteem and maintains a state of quiet insecurity. Therapy helps you observe this critical voice without accepting its harsh conclusions.

Somatic tracking and relational healing for shame

Because shame lands so distinctly in the physical body, somatic work offers a powerful pathway to relief. Using techniques from Somatic Experiencing, we pay gentle attention to where shame resides physically, helping your nervous system release the urge to collapse or hide, and restoring a feeling of upright, grounded stability.

Relational healing occurs when shame is brought safely into a non-judgmental environment. Drawing on person-centred therapy, I offer steady empathy and unconditional positive regard, meeting all of your experiences without evaluation. Experiencing consistent acceptance within our therapeutic relationship helps deconstruct the deep-seated belief that you are fundamentally unworthy, broken, or unlovable.

Questions about therapy for shame and self-esteem

How can somatic therapy help with feelings of shame?

Shame triggers a physiological collapse response, such as pulling shoulders inward or dropping eye contact. Somatic therapy notices these physical cues and supports your body to complete stuck protective responses, slowly building a physical sense of safety, presence, and worth.

What is the difference between low self-esteem and shame?

Low self-esteem usually involves doubting your abilities or worth in specific situations. Shame is a deeper, core feeling that you are inherently broken or unworthy as a person. Therapy addresses both by untangling negative self-beliefs and building compassionate self-acceptance.
