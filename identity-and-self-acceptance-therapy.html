Conditions of worth and the masks we wear

In person-centred therapy, Carl Rogers introduced the idea of conditions of worth: the implicit lessons we learn growing up that we are only acceptable when we behave, succeed, or feel a certain way. To maintain love and approval, we construct protective masks, adapting our behaviour to satisfy external expectations while burying our true desires.

While people-pleasing and mask-wearing begin as sensible survival strategies, they come at a heavy cost over time. You might find yourself feeling disconnected from your own opinions, uncertain of your personal boundaries, or deeply exhausted by everyday social interactions. Recognizing these learned protective patterns is the essential first step toward reclaiming your authentic identity.

Rediscovering who you are in a non-judgmental space

My approach rests on core person-centred principles: empathy, congruence, and unconditional positive regard. In our sessions, there are no expectations to perform or fulfill a role. For those exploring gender or sexuality, my practice is LGBTQ+ affirming, rooted in lived experience as a gay man where there is no need to educate your therapist.

We also integrate body awareness to notice how self-suppression manifests physically. You might notice a constricted throat when holding back your truth, or tightness in the stomach when saying yes to something you dislike. Somatic tracking helps you tune in to these body signals, grounding self-acceptance in felt experience rather than abstract ideas.

Questions about identity and self-acceptance therapy

What does conditions of worth mean in therapy?

Conditions of worth refer to the beliefs we internalize that we are only worthy of love or respect when meeting certain criteria, such as pleasing others or suppressing our true feelings. Therapy helps you identify and untangle these unspoken rules so you can accept yourself unconditionally.

Is identity therapy only for LGBTQ+ clients?

No. While I bring lived experience as a gay therapist and offer LGBTQ+ affirming care, identity work is for anyone who feels disconnected from themselves, struggles with people-pleasing, or feels trapped in social roles. I work with adult clients from all backgrounds across the UK.

How can therapy help me stop people-pleasing?

People-pleasing is often a habitual way to secure safety and approval. In therapy, we explore the feelings that arise when you set boundaries or say no. By building somatic grounding and self-compassion, you learn to honor your own needs without fear of abandonment.
