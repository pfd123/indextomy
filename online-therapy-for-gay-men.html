Understanding the distinct experiences of gay men

Gay men often navigate complex layers of social expectation, hypervigilance, and subtle isolation. Growing up in environments that praise heteronormativity can leave lasting marks, even after achieving external success or living openly. Many men find themselves carrying an implicit sense of needing to perform, prove their worth, or keep their true feelings guarded. In our work together, we create a space to slow down and unpack these pressures without judgment.

Coming out is rarely a single event; it is an ongoing process that happens at different life stages. Whether you came out in your teens or are exploring your sexuality later in adulthood, the transition brings profound questions about identity, family dynamics, and belonging. Therapy allows you to process these changes safely, letting go of long-held fear and building a self-directed, authentic life.

Shared lived experience and body aware support

Working with a therapist who shares your lived experience as a gay man creates an immediate foundation of trust and understanding. You do not need to explain the nuances of gay dating culture, social dynamics, or minority stress. You can explore broader topics on my LGBTQ+ affirming therapy page, or see how I work with self-worth on my therapy for shame and self-esteem page.

My approach combines person-centred therapy with Somatic Experiencing to address emotional difficulties at both a psychological and physical level. Internalized shame frequently manifests as physical tension, chronic anxiety, or difficulty feeling present in intimacy. By working gently with bodily sensations alongside your spoken narrative, we help your nervous system release old protective habits and welcome a deeper sense of ease and self-acceptance.

Questions about therapy for gay men

Why choose a gay therapist for online therapy?

Working with a therapist who shares your lived experience means you do not have to explain gay cultural norms or minority stress. It allows us to establish immediate rapport and focus directly on your emotional well-being.

Can therapy help if I am coming out later in life?

Yes. Coming out later in adulthood involves navigating existing family, marriage, or career structures. Therapy provides a thoughtful, non-judgmental space to process your feelings, honour your identity, and make decisions at your own pace.

What happens during the free introductory call?

The free 20 minute call is a quiet conversation over video or phone. We discuss what brings you to therapy, answer any practical questions about fees or scheduling, and decide together if my approach is the right fit for you.
