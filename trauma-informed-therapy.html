What trauma-informed therapy means in practice

A trauma-informed approach prioritises personal safety, clear consent, and gentle boundaries. Traditional talking therapy can sometimes create an implicit expectation to retell painful stories in detail. In my practice, establishing physical and emotional safety comes first. You will never be pushed to recount overwhelming events before feeling grounded and ready, allowing us to move strictly at a pace your system can manage.

Trauma influences how your nervous system perceives safety and threat. Following overwhelming experiences, the mind may seek to move forward while the body continues to hold protective bracing. A trauma-informed model recognises that hypervigilance, muscle tightness, or numbness are protective strategies rather than personal shortcomings. We respect these bodily responses rather than attempting to force rapid change.

How Somatic Experiencing addresses stuck survival responses

Developed by Dr Peter Levine, Somatic Experiencing explores how stress and traumatic experiences are held in the body. When a threat arises, the nervous system naturally prepares to fight, flee, or freeze. If those self-protective responses are interrupted, physical tension, ongoing bracing, or an underlying sense of anxiety can remain stored in bodily patterns over time.

By noticing physical sensations such as warmth, muscle tightness, or subtle changes in breath, somatic therapy invites a gentle exploration of bodily tension without overwhelming your system. Combining body awareness with a person-centred foundation, I offer empathy and steady presence, creating a secure relational environment where your nervous system may gradually find greater physical ease.

Questions about trauma-informed somatic therapy

Do I have to talk about my past trauma in detail?

No. Trauma-informed somatic therapy does not require you to revisit or retell painful memories to process them. Instead, we pay attention to how your nervous system holds tension in the present moment, working safely within your comfort level so you stay grounded throughout our work.

How does somatic work help with feeling hypervigilant or on edge?

Hypervigilance reflects a nervous system remaining alert to keep you safe. Somatic Experiencing tracks subtle physical cues like muscular tension and breathing patterns, helping your system explore gentle grounding so you can notice whether the present environment feels safer than past experiences.

Is online trauma-informed therapy effective?

Yes. Online therapy allows you to stay in your own familiar space where you already feel comfortable. Video sessions offer the relational connection needed for somatic work, allowing us to attend to bodily cues, pacing, and grounding just as attentively as in-person sessions.
