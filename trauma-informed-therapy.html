What trauma-informed therapy means in practice

A trauma-informed approach begins with safety, consent, and clear boundaries. In traditional talking therapy, there can be an unspoken pressure to recount painful events in detail. In my practice, safety comes first. You will never be pushed to retell difficult stories before you feel fully grounded and ready. We move strictly at a pace that respects your system.

Trauma changes how your nervous system assesses threat. When overwhelming events occur, the conscious mind might move forward while the body remains braced for impact. A trauma-informed model recognizes that hypervigilance and numbness are protective mechanisms, not flaws. We honour those protective responses rather than trying to strip them away forcibly.

How Somatic Experiencing addresses stuck survival responses

Developed by Dr Peter Levine, Somatic Experiencing focuses on how stress and trauma are held in the physical body. When a threat occurs, the body prepares to fight, flee, or freeze. If that self-protective energy cannot complete its cycle, it stays trapped as physical tension, chronic bracing, or an underlying feeling of dread.

By tracking physical sensations such as warmth, muscle tension, or subtle shifts in breathing, somatic work helps safely release trapped survival energy without causing overwhelm. Combining this body awareness with a person-centred foundation, I offer unconditional positive regard and steady empathy, creating a secure relational anchor as your nervous system gradually learns to settle.

Questions about trauma-informed somatic therapy

Do I have to talk about my past trauma in detail?

No. Trauma-informed somatic therapy does not require you to revisit or retell painful memories to heal. Instead, we focus on how your nervous system holds the trauma in the present moment. We work safely within your window of tolerance, keeping you grounded throughout every session.

How does somatic work help with feeling hypervigilant or on edge?

Hypervigilance is your nervous system remaining on high alert to protect you from harm. Somatic Experiencing tracks subtle physical cues like muscle tension and breath patterns, helping your nervous system discharge stuck survival energy and gradually learn that the current moment is safe.

Is online trauma-informed therapy effective?

Yes, online therapy allows you to remain in your own comfortable space where you already feel secure. Video sessions offer the full relational presence needed for somatic work, allowing us to attend to body cues, pacing, and grounding just as effectively as in person.
